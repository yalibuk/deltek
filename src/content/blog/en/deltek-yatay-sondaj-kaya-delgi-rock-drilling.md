The video above shows the Deltek crew carrying out **horizontal directional
drilling in rock**. Rock drilling is the most demanding HDD application: the
steering done with a duckbill head in soft ground does not work in hard
formations. Instead, a **mud motor** driven by the pressure of the drilling
fluid and the tricone bit at its tip are used; the bit turns while the rods do
not, and direction is given by the angled motor body (bend sub).

## What matters in rock drilling?

- **Fluid flow and pressure:** A mud motor needs at least 500 litres of
  drilling fluid per minute; the mud pump and recycling unit are sized
  accordingly.
- **Bit and reamer selection:** Carbide or diamond-tipped bits according to
  the compressive strength of the rock, and a cone-cutter **rock reamer** for
  enlargement. The number of reaming stages is higher than in soft ground.
- **Locating:** Signal transmission is weaker in rock formations; on deep and
  long crossings a wireline [magnetic guidance](/en/magnetic-guidance/) system is
  preferred.
- **Time and cost:** The rate of advance in rock is lower than in soft ground
  and bit and reamer wear is higher. The right choice of rig and equipment keeps
  the project on schedule and on budget.
- **Torque and rig class:** In rock the decisive figure is torque capacity,
  not pull force; rig selection is based on the compressive strength in the
  geotechnical survey.
- **Mud recipe:** Rock cuttings are heavy; the viscosity of the drilling fluid
  is raised with polymer additives so that it can carry them out.

## Which projects require rock drilling?

In Türkiye, the limestone formations along the Aegean and Mediterranean
coasts, the volcanic rocks of Central Anatolia and the weathered rock of the
Marmara region in particular require rock drilling on HDD projects. Bedrock
beneath river beds and the hard layers beneath motorway and railway embankments
fall into the same category.
